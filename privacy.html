Dicticus Privacy Policy

Last updated: 31 August 2026

Dicticus does not collect any data. Your voice recordings and the text transcribed from them stay on your device. Nothing is sent to us, and nothing is sent to anyone else.

How your voice is handled

Dicticus turns speech into text entirely on your own device. Both the speech recognition model and the optional text cleanup model run locally, on your device's own processor. Your audio is never uploaded anywhere in order to be transcribed, and once the transcription is finished the recording is discarded rather than kept.

This is not a policy choice that could quietly change in an update. It is how the app is built: there is no server on our side that could receive your voice, because we do not operate one.

What is stored, and where

Everything Dicticus keeps is stored on your device alone. That includes your transcription history, so that you can find and reuse what you dictated earlier, and you are free to delete individual entries or clear the history entirely. It also includes any word corrections you add to your personal dictionary, your app settings, and the speech recognition model itself, along with the text cleanup model if you have chosen to enable it.

None of this is transmitted anywhere, backed up to our servers, or visible to us in any form.

When Dicticus uses the network

There are exactly two occasions, and neither one involves your voice or your text. The first is downloading the models: when you first use the app it fetches the speech recognition model, and the text cleanup model as well if you enable that feature, from public model repositories. The second, on macOS only, is checking a public feed to see whether a newer version of the app has been released.

Once the models are on your device, Dicticus works completely offline.

Analytics and tracking

There are none of either. Dicticus contains no analytics library, no crash reporting service, no advertising identifier, and no tracking of any kind. We have no way of knowing who you are, whether you use the app, or how often you open it.

Sharing your data

We do not share your data with anyone, for the straightforward reason that we never receive it. Dicticus has no accounts, no sign in, and no server component that holds anything belonging to you.

The permissions Dicticus asks for

  • Microphone access, which the app needs in order to hear you at all. This one is required.
  • Accessibility and Input Monitoring on macOS, so that the push to talk hotkey works in any application and so that the finished text can be placed at your cursor.
  • Audio capture from other apps on macOS, which is optional. Dicticus uses it for one narrow purpose: measuring whether sound is currently playing, so that it can pause your music while you dictate. Only the loudness level is read. No audio from other apps is ever recorded, stored, or examined for its content.

Children

Dicticus is not aimed at children, and it collects no information from anyone at all, children included.

If this policy ever changes

Should Dicticus ever gain a feature that sends audio or text off your device, this page will be updated to say so before that feature ships, and the change will be described here in plain terms rather than buried in a list of amendments. Any such feature would be switched off by default and would require your explicit consent before it did anything.

Getting in touch
