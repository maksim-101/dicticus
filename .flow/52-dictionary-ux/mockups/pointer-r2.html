Board 1 of 2, round 2: marking a word is the first half of the dictionary entry

Question: after a dictation with a misheard word, how should Dicticus lead you to the dictionary?

What changed since round 1: the owner rejected all three variants. Asked for: “first of all see where I’m at, what’s the last transcription, which transcription did I just choose, and then I mark a word or a few words and just the marking starts the first half of the dictionary entry, and a text field will appear where I can add the corrected value.” So: no “Add to Dictionary…” button and no sheet; no Raw/Polished switch (you always judge the polished text) and no Revert to Raw; marking one word, or dragging or shift-clicking across a few, fills “Heard as” and opens an inline “Correct spelling” field under the text with the cursor in it. Return adds, Escape cancels, and a confirmation line with Undo follows.

Direction: native macOS Sonoma/Tahoe window and popover, system font, dense list; signature element: the heard-as chips (here the “Heard as” chip in the inline field). Each variant is a 3-step sequence in its real frame. Anything the app does not have yet carries NEW. Invented names only; the confirmation never rewrites the past dictation text.

A — In the popover

Differs in: the Home card is the place: titled “Last dictation · 12:14 · EN” with a blue outline so it is clear which dictation you are on. Its polished text can be marked in place, and a small “‹ earlier” control steps to older dictations. Revert to Raw is gone; Copy is the only button. No Add to Dictionary button, no sheet.

Cost: medium (a markable text view in the popover that reports the marked range, an inline field, the step-back state). Limit: a 350 px wide popover, so long dictations get tall; no window. Clicks: 0 extra to reach it, then mark, type, Return.

Constraint: the heard form comes from the polished text. The app’s post-cleanup dictionary pass applies user entries to the processed text, so an entry made from a polished word does work.

B — Fix window, two columns

Differs in: one small window “Fix a word”. A list of recent dictations on the left, the last one preselected and highlighted; the chosen dictation large on the right, markable, with the field under it. Reached by one new item on the popover’s Home (“Fix a word…” beside Copy) or from the History tab.

Cost: medium to large (new window, list, markable large text, inline field). Room: shows four dictations at once, so older ones are one click away. Clicks: 1 to open from the popover, then mark, type, Return.

Constraint: the heard form comes from the polished text. The app’s post-cleanup dictionary pass applies user entries to the processed text, so an entry made from a polished word does work.

C — Fix window, one column with a picker (my proposal)

Differs in: B without the list. The dictation fills the window; a picker in the header (‹ › and a menu with the recent ones, “Last dictation” on top) says which one you are on. Nothing competes with the text and the field, so it is the calmest of the three. Opened like B.

Cost: medium (B’s window minus the list, plus a picker). Trade-off: older dictations are reached through the picker, not seen at once, but you only judge one dictation at a time anyway. Clicks: 1 to open, then mark, type, Return.

Constraint: the heard form comes from the polished text. The app’s post-cleanup dictionary pass applies user entries to the processed text, so an entry made from a polished word does work.
