Board 2 of 2 — picking a misheard word in History and adding it to the dictionary (clickable)

Direction: the existing History window (macOS HistoryView): rows with date, language and mode tags, a chevron that opens the Raw / Polished picker and the full text. Only the way a word is picked differs between A and B; both open the same sheet. NEW marks what the app does not have today.

Try it: in each window the first dictation is opened on Raw. Pick "Molly do" and add it as Moleido (a word that already has three heard forms, so it joins that row). Then pick "cloud" in the second dictation to see both warnings. The dictations are invented; the dictionary behind the sheet is a small fake one.

Rules common to both: picking works only in the Raw view, since the recognizer's own output is what the dictionary matches against; Polished text has already been changed by the dictionary and the AI step. Punctuation at the ends of a pick is dropped. The new entry applies from the next dictation on; past dictations stay as they were.

A — click words

Words in the Raw text highlight on hover; click one to pick it, shift-click another to extend the pick to a run of words. An "Add to Dictionary…" button appears under the text. Built from plain SwiftUI word views.

B — select and right-click

The Raw text selects like any Mac text: drag across words, then right-click and choose "Add to Dictionary…" from the usual menu. Needs an AppKit text view in place of today's SwiftUI text, so the app can read the selection.

What this board covers and leaves out: the pick and the sheet, in the full History window. Not drawn: the menu-bar popover's History tab (it shows only the polished text of recent dictations, so it gets no picking); a keyboard shortcut for the sheet; editing a past dictation's text. The sheet's suggestions list only shows spellings already in the dictionary.
